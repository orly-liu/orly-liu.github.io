Methodology

This methodology is adaptable to any asset-intensive industry where equipment downtime carries a quantifiable financial penalty, such as manufacturing, mining, aviation, logistics, and energy sectors. By integrating inventory economics, operational risk, and demand probability, the calculator recommends optimal stocking levels to maximize asset availability while minimizing capital investment.

Economic Order Quantity

Economic Order Quantity estimates the replenishment batch size that balances annual ordering expense against annual inventory holding expense. Higher ordering costs or annual demand increase the EOQ, while higher unit prices or holding rates decrease it.

EOQ = ⌈√2 × Annual Demand × Fixed Ordering CostUnit Price × Annual Holding Rate⌉

Expected Demand During Lead Time

Annual Demand is converted into the expected number of units required while a replenishment order is in transit. Represented as the Poisson mean (λ), the risk during this exposure period scales with longer lead times and higher annual demand.

λ = Annual Demand × Lead Time365

Overstock Cost

This is the annual penalty for holding one additional unit of safety stock. It captures the impact of tied-up capital, which may include storage, insurance, depreciation, and obsolescence. High-value parts or high holding rates increase this cost.

Overstock Cost = Unit Price × Annual Holding Rate

Understock Cost

This is the annualized financial consequence of having one less unit of safety stock. Inventory is typically vulnerable to stockouts only during the lead time. Therefore, higher downtime costs, longer lead times, or more frequent ordering increases this cost.

Understock Cost = Downtime Cost Per Day × Lead Time × (Annual Demand ÷ EOQ)⌈λ⌉

Critical Ratio & Service Level

The Critical Ratio compares the cost of understocking with the combined costs of understocking and overstocking, establishing an economically justified target service level. When downtime is significantly more expensive than carrying excess stock, this ratio naturally approaches 100%.

Critical Ratio = Understock CostUnderstock Cost + Overstock Cost

Poisson Demand Distribution

To model the discrete occurrences of demand during the lead time, the calculator uses a Poisson distribution. The cumulative probability measures the likelihood that actual lead-time demand will be less than or equal to a candidate stocking quantity (k).

P(X ≤ k) = ∑i=0k e−λ λii!

Reorder Point (ROP) & Maximum Stock

The calculator evaluates the Poisson cumulative distribution to find the smallest whole-unit quantity whose cumulative probability meets or exceeds the Critical Ratio. This baseline becomes the Reorder Point. The maximum recommended stock level is simply the ROP plus the EOQ.

ROP = min { k ∈ ℤ≥0 : P(X ≤ k | λ) ≥ Critical Ratio }

On Hand Comparison

Current on-hand quantity is compared with the recommended range. Understock: on-hand quantity is below the reorder point. Within range: on-hand quantity is between the reorder point and maximum stock. Overstock: on-hand quantity exceeds the maximum stock.

Assumptions: Demand events are independent and Poisson-distributed with a stable average rate. Lead time is fixed, cost inputs are current, and recommended quantities are rounded to whole numbers. An order is placed as soon as inventory falls to or below the reorder point, for the exact quantity determined by the EOQ. The calculator is a decision-support tool and should be reviewed alongside operational judgment, part criticality, supplier reliability, shelf life, and buying policies.
